A drawing is a record of design intent. Before using it to plan work, take a moment to confirm that everyone is working from the same information.

## Start with the title block

Check the drawing number, title, status and revision. Compare them with the document register and the issue you have been instructed to use. A newer file in your inbox does not automatically mean it is approved for construction.

## Follow the dimensions

Read stated dimensions and levels carefully. Avoid measuring from a screen or a printed image where a dimension is missing. If a level or dimension conflicts with another drawing, record the question before setting out.

## Check the interfaces

Look beyond the element shown on the sheet. A wall may affect a service route; a beam opening may affect reinforcement; a finish may affect a door clearance. Check relevant plans, sections, details and specifications together.

## Record what remains unclear

Note the exact drawing reference, revision, location and decision needed. Raise the query through your project's agreed process and keep the response with the working records. That makes the next conversation easier and leaves a trace of the decision.

This is a general reading method. Always follow the approved documents and procedures for your project.
